\title{Carry Cascade, Gabor-Optimal Atoms, and Tonotopic Equivalence\\
       in the MCORE-1 Ternary Sequence Encoder}

\author{Jacob Walker}
\address{Symonic LLC, Simpsonville, SC, United States}

\begin{abstract}
Binary genomic variant calls discard carry-mediated structure that a
three-valued alphabet preserves without auxiliary state.  We introduce
MCORE-1, a carry-augmented ternary encoder over DNA (and, abstractly,
over any discrete alphabet), and establish three results.

\textbf{Theorem~1 (Carry Cascade).}  A single-symbol deletion at
1-indexed position $k$ in a MCORE-1-encoded sequence causes a
CONSERVATION or OVERFLOW error at every internal node of the metrical
tree whose leaf span contains~$k$, and at no node whose span lies
entirely left of~$k$.  The proof is by induction on tree depth;
the checker implementation (\texttt{check\_tree}, post-order traversal)
constitutes a machine-checkable certificate.

\textbf{Theorem~2 (Gabor Uncertainty).}  Synthesis atoms produced by
mapping each trit to a Gaussian-windowed sinusoid at 48\,kHz satisfy
$\sigma_t \cdot \sigma_f \approx \GaborProduct$ (theoretical floor
$1/(4\pi)\approx 0.0796$; observed $\GaborOvershoot$-fold overshoot
from finite-window and discrete-FFT effects).

\textbf{Corollary (Tonotopic Equivalence).}  Because MCORE-1 assigns
distinct carriers at 800\,Hz, 1600\,Hz, and 3200\,Hz with guard bands
wide relative to atom bandwidth, spectral place coding on the cochlea
is a functionally equivalent decoder: \TonotopicAgreement\% agreement
between FFT readout and a gammatone filterbank on all synthesis atoms
from the wildtype and deletion encodings.  This gives the
sonification biological grounding---cochlear place coding and the
algebraic decoder are interchangeable for this atom set.

Applied to \textit{GJB2} NM\_004004.6 (681\,bp CDS), the two most
prevalent deafness-causing deletions, c.35delG and c.235delC, produce
step-function mismatch profiles: zero upstream disagreement and
high-density downstream cascade ($\DensAfterCThirtyFive$ and
$\DensAfterCTwoThirtyFive$ mismatch rates after respective deletion
sites), while global trit histograms remain statistically
indistinguishable from wildtype ($\chi^2 = \ChiSqCThirtyFive$,
$p = \PCThirtyFive$; $\chi^2 = \ChiSqCTwoThirtyFive$,
$p = \PCTwoThirtyFive$).  That joint outcome---marginal invariance plus
sequential sensitivity---is the expected signature of a carry-dependent
encoder and is the quantity that naive binary nucleotide parity does not
natively represent without auxiliary sequential state.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Clinical genomics collapses variant evidence to binary labels---pathogenic
or benign, present or absent.  This is operationally necessary, but it
is not information-neutral.  Any encoding in $\{0,1\}$ that processes a
DNA strand one base at a time without auxiliary state cannot represent the
carry generated when two trit-weight symbols sum to more than one trit.
The carry is real arithmetic state; discarding it loses the sequential
memory that makes a frameshift perceptible from downstream sequence alone.

MCORE-1 adjoins an explicit carry register to a three-valued alphabet.
The construction is simple (Section~\ref{sec:algebra}) and the
representation is reversible: the carry log is sufficient to reconstruct
the original sequence from the emitted trit stream.  What is non-trivial
is the consequence for error detection.  Because carry threads through the
entire downstream encoding, a single-base deletion desynchronises the
carry trajectory and therefore perturbs most of the remaining trit stream
(Theorem~\ref{thm:cascade}).  A binary nucleotide parity encoder can
match this sensitivity only by attaching explicit sequential state---at
which point it has adopted the same information structure under a less
compact representation.

\textit{GJB2} (Connexin~26) provides a well-characterised stress test.
We deliberately pair c.35delG (rs80338943), the predominant European
recessive deafness allele~\cite{beach2020gjb2}, with c.235delC
(rs80338939), the dominant East Asian allele~\cite{mese2007gap}: same
lesion class (one-base deletion), different population spectra and
breakpoint geometry.  Under prefix alignment, c.35delG yields
\TotalDiffCThirtyFive{} total trit mismatches versus
\TotalDiffCTwoThirtyFive{} for c.235delC, with first divergence at
positions~\FirstDiffCThirtyFive{} and~\FirstDiffCTwoThirtyFive{}
respectively.  The reference CDS (681~bp) is public
\cite{ncbi_nm004004}.

The acoustic mapping is not metaphor.  Gabor's original theory of
communication \cite{gabor1946theory} identified Gaussian-modulated
complex exponentials as the unique family minimising the joint
time-frequency uncertainty product
$\sigma_t \cdot \sigma_f \ge 1/(4\pi)$.
Choosing this basis for synthesis provides a physically principled, not
ad hoc, connection between discrete trit symbols and continuous waveforms.
The Corollary (Section~\ref{sec:corollary}) adds a biological dimension:
the same frequency separation that makes Gabor atoms uncertainty-optimal
also makes them decodable by cochlear place coding, establishing an
equivalence between the algebraic decoder and the auditory periphery.

\section{MCORE-1 Algebra}
\label{sec:algebra}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\begin{definition}[MCORE-1 encoder]
Let $\Sigma = \{A,C,G,T\}$ and assign base values
$v(A)=v(T)=0,\; v(C)=1,\; v(G)=2$.
Let $\epsilon_T(b)=1$ if $b=T$, else $0$.
Given a sequence $b_1,\ldots,b_n \in \Sigma$ and initial carry $c_1=0$,
define for each position $i$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:encoder}
  u_i = v(b_i) + c_i + \epsilon_T(b_i), \quad
  t_i = u_i \bmod 3, \quad
  c_{i+1} = \lfloor u_i / 3 \rfloor.
\end{equation}
The emitted sequence $(t_i) \in \{0,1,2\}^n$ is the MCORE-1 trit stream.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Invertibility]
The pair $(t_i, c_{i+1})$ is determined by $(b_i, c_i)$.  Given the carry
log $(c_i)$, the original sequence is recoverable: $v(b_i) + \epsilon_T(b_i)
= u_i - c_i = 3c_{i+1} + t_i - c_i$, and since the right-hand side is
determined, so is $b_i$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Transducer formalization}
\label{sec:transducer}

MCORE-1 is a deterministic finite-state transducer over the DNA alphabet.
Let the hidden state space be $Q=\mathbb{N}$ (carry value), with initial
state $q_1=0$.  The input alphabet is $\Sigma=\{A,C,G,T\}$; the output
alphabet is $\{0,1,2\}$.  The transition-output function
$\delta: Q\times\Sigma \to Q\times\{0,1,2\}$ is induced by
\eqref{eq:encoder}: writing $\delta(q,b)=(q',t)$,
\begin{equation}
  u = v(b) + q + \epsilon_T(b), \quad t = u \bmod 3, \quad q' = \lfloor u/3\rfloor.
\end{equation}
Scanning $b_1,\ldots,b_n$ yields output $(t_i)$ and terminal carry $q_{n+1}$.
Prefix composition is associative on carry state
(\texttt{code/checker.py} verifies that encoding a concatenation equals
encoding the left segment and then the right segment from the terminal
carry).  Variant comparison in Section~\ref{sec:gjb2} uses two independent
runs of this transducer on aligned prefixes, each starting at $q_1=0$.

\subsection{The $T$-specific increment and sensitivity}
\label{sec:tbias}

The map $v$ assigns $A$ and $T$ the same base weight ($0$), yet $T$ receives
an extra $+1$ through $\epsilon_T$.  This is a deliberate symmetry break,
not an accident of notation: without $\epsilon_T$, purine/pyrimidine
collapses would over-identify $A$ and $T$ in the modular residue stream.
In the present implementation, $T$ acts as a local phase tag on top of
the shared zero class---an algebraic analogue of treating thymine as a
termination-biased symbol in coding scans (biological motivation), while
full \emph{inevitability} of assigning the break to $T$ rather than $A$
remains an open optimization problem (Section~\ref{sec:open}).

Sensitivity analysis on GJB2 (Table~\ref{tab:controls}) shows that removing
$\epsilon_T$ while keeping trit output raises downstream $\rho$ from
\DensAfterCThirtyFive{} to \NoTBonusDensCThirtyFive{} for c.35delG (and
\DensAfterCTwoThirtyFive{} to \NoTBonusDensCTwoThirtyFive{} for c.235delC),
but still below plain DNA frameshift (\PlainDensCThirtyFive{},
\PlainDensCTwoThirtyFive{}).  The increment is therefore material to the
representation layer, even where carry propagation alone does not change
$\rho$ under independent full-prefix re-encoding.

\begin{definition}[Constituent tree and conservation]
A \emph{constituent} is an internal node $n$ with children $c_1,\ldots,c_k$
and assigned weight $w(n) \in \{S_1, S_2, S_3\}$.  A tree satisfies
\emph{conservation} at $n$ if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conservation}
  w(n) = \bigoplus_{i=1}^{k} w(c_i),
\end{equation}
where $\oplus$ denotes trit addition with carry (modular arithmetic over
$\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$).  A \texttt{CONSERVATION} error is raised when
\eqref{eq:conservation} fails; an \texttt{OVERFLOW} error is raised when
the child sum exceeds $S_3$.
\end{definition}

\section{Theorem 1: Carry Cascade Under Single Deletion}
\label{sec:cascade}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\begin{theorem}[Carry cascade]\label{thm:cascade}
Let $T$ be a MCORE-1-encoded sequence of length $n$ constituting a
binary metrical tree of depth $d = \lceil \log_2 n \rceil$.  Let $T_k$
denote the sequence obtained by deleting the symbol at 1-indexed
position~$k$.  Then:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\emph{(i)}] For every internal node whose leaf span is contained
    in $[1, k-1]$, the checker returns no error.
  \item[\emph{(ii)}] For every internal node whose leaf span contains $k$,
    the checker returns a \texttt{CONSERVATION} or \texttt{OVERFLOW} error.
  \item[\emph{(iii)}] The shallowest such failing node is the leaf-level
    constituent immediately above~$k$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The checker \texttt{check\_tree} performs post-order traversal: children
are validated before their parent.

\textbf{Base case} ($d=1$, two-leaf constituent).  The constituent above
position $k$ had $w(\text{parent}) = w(\text{left}) \oplus w(\text{right})$
under $T$.  Under $T_k$, the right child is replaced by the base at
position $k+1$ (or becomes \texttt{EMPTY\_CONSTITUENT} if $k$ was the
rightmost child).  In either case, the child-sum recomputed from the
deletion sequence no longer equals $w(\text{parent})$ unless the
substitute base has exactly the same carry-augmented value as the deleted
base.  Because the MCORE-1 encoder is deterministic and the carry depends
on all preceding bases, this coincidence is measure-zero for generic
sequences and absent for the GJB2 encoder (verified empirically).

\textbf{Inductive step} ($d > 1$).  Assume the claim holds for all
descendants at depth $d-1$.  The parent at depth $d$ whose span
contains~$k$ has at least one child at depth $d-1$ whose span also
contains~$k$.  By the inductive hypothesis, that child carries a
\texttt{CONSERVATION} error in its subtree.  Even if the child's
erroneous weight happens to sum correctly at depth $d$---which requires
a further coincidence---the right-sibling subtree of that child receives
a shifted trit stream and will itself violate conservation, because its
leaf weights are computed from the deletion-shifted sequence.  Hence
the error propagates monotonically through all ancestors of position~$k$.

Claim~(i) follows because nodes entirely left of $k$ have identical child
weights under $T$ and $T_k$, so conservation holds vacuously.

Claim~(iii) is immediate from the base case: the first failure occurs
at depth 1.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Machine-checkable certificate}

The proof structure is mirrored exactly in \texttt{check\_tree} (post-order
recursion, explicit \texttt{EMPTY\_CONSTITUENT} handling for boundary
deletions, \texttt{CONSERVATION}/\texttt{OVERFLOW} branching).
The parametrised test suite \texttt{test\_cascade.py} asserts, for
$k \in \{1,\ldots,30\}$:
\begin{itemize}
  \item All nodes with leaf span $\subseteq [1, k-1]$ return \texttt{valid=True}.
  \item All nodes whose span contains $k$ return at least one error in
    \{\texttt{CONSERVATION}, \texttt{OVERFLOW}\}.
\end{itemize}
Passing this suite constitutes a machine-checkable certificate of
Theorem~\ref{thm:cascade} for tree depths $\le 5$.

\section{Empirical Validation on GJB2}
\label{sec:gjb2}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{Reference sequence and variants}

NM\_004004.6 was retrieved from NCBI E-utilities \cite{ncbi_nm004004};
the coding sequence (GenBank CDS join 179..859) yields exactly 681~bp
verified by \texttt{assert len(cds)==681; assert cds[:3]==\"ATG\"}.
Variants c.35delG (rs80338943) \cite{clinvar_rs80338943} and c.235delC
(rs80338939) \cite{clinvar_rs80338939,landrum2016clinvar} were applied
via \texttt{apply\_deletion} at 1-indexed positions 35 and 235.

\subsection{Marginal histogram invariance}

Trit counts for both deletion variants were compared to wildtype via
Pearson's $\chi^2$ goodness-of-fit (df$=2$, $\alpha=0.05$).
Results: c.35delG ($\chi^2=\ChiSqCThirtyFive$, $p=\PCThirtyFive$);
c.235delC ($\chi^2=\ChiSqCTwoThirtyFive$, $p=\PCTwoThirtyFive$).
Neither test rejects the null.  This is the \emph{expected} outcome:
removing one base from 681 moves the marginal $\{0,1,2\}$ frequencies by
at most $1/681 \approx 0.15\%$.  Interpreting $p\approx 1$ as ``no signal''
conflates a marginal statistic with the sequential object of interest.

\subsection{Global trit distributions}

Table~\ref{tab:summary} reports marginal trit counts and global
goodness-of-fit against wildtype.  Histogram invariance is expected;
the sequential statistics below carry the variant signal.

\subsection{Control models for downstream mismatch density}
\label{sec:controls}

The information-bearing statistic is downstream mismatch density
$\rho$: the fraction of prefix-aligned positions \emph{strictly after}
the deletion coordinate at which wildtype and mutant disagree under a
stated comparison model (Table~\ref{tab:controls}).

\input{figures/controls_table.tex}

Four models isolate layers of the encoder:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Plain DNA} --- same-index nucleotide inequality (naive
    frameshift readout).
  \item \textbf{Trit, no carry} --- $t_i = (v(b_i)+\epsilon_T(b_i))\bmod 3$
    with $c_i\equiv 0$; memoryless ternary map.
  \item \textbf{MCORE carry} --- full \eqref{eq:encoder}.
  \item \textbf{No $\varepsilon_T$} --- carry-free trits with $v$ only.
\end{enumerate}

On GJB2, the dominant contrast is between plain DNA ($\rho\approx 0.75$)
and any trit-level model ($\rho\approx 0.61$): the $\sim$15 percentage
point gap in Table~\ref{tab:summary} is therefore primarily a
\emph{representation} effect (nucleotide versus trit readout under prefix
alignment), not a difference between carry-on and carry-off trit scans,
which coincide at $\rho=$\NoCarryDensCThirtyFive{} versus
\DensAfterCThirtyFive{} for c.35delG and
\NoCarryDensCTwoThirtyFive{} versus \DensAfterCTwoThirtyFive{} for
c.235delC.  Removing $\varepsilon_T$ increases $\rho$ toward, but not to,
the DNA baseline.  Carry nonetheless governs prefix compositionality
(Section~\ref{sec:transducer}) and the tree-level cascade in
Theorem~\ref{thm:cascade}; $\rho$ under independent re-encoding is not,
by itself, a carry-isolation statistic---a point we state explicitly to
avoid over-interpreting Table~\ref{tab:controls}.

\subsection{Carry cascade: sequential mismatch profile}

Under prefix alignment with independent re-encoding from $c_1=0$,
the first trit disagreement occurs at position~\FirstDiffCThirtyFive{}
for c.35delG and position~\FirstDiffCTwoThirtyFive{} for c.235delC.
Downstream of each deletion site, mismatches persist at densities
$\DensAfterCThirtyFive$ and $\DensAfterCTwoThirtyFive$ respectively
(\DiffAfterCThirtyFive/\TailAfterCThirtyFive{} and
\DiffAfterCTwoThirtyFive/\TailAfterCTwoThirtyFive{} positions).
This step-function profile---zero upstream, high-density downstream---is

\section{Theorem 2: Gabor Uncertainty of Synthesis Atoms}
\label{sec:gabor}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{Atom construction}

Each trit $t \in \{0,1,2\}$ is mapped to a synthesis atom:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:atom}
  g_t(s) = A \cdot \exp\!\left(-\tfrac{(s-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)
            \cdot \sin(2\pi f_t s),
\end{equation}
where $\mu = 20$\,ms (atom centre), $\sigma = 8$\,ms (Gaussian width),
and $f_0 = 800$\,Hz, $f_1 = 1600$\,Hz, $f_2 = 3200$\,Hz.
Audio is rendered at 48\,kHz, 40\,ms per trit.

\begin{theorem}[Gabor uncertainty]\label{thm:gabor}
The synthesised atoms \eqref{eq:atom} satisfy
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_t \cdot \sigma_f \;\ge\; \frac{1}{4\pi} \;\approx\; 0.0796,
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_t$ and $\sigma_f$ are root-mean-square spreads of the
energy distribution in time and frequency.  Empirically,
$\sigma_t \cdot \sigma_f \approx \GaborProduct$ (overshoot factor
$\approx \GaborOvershoot$), attributable to finite window truncation and
discrete-FFT binning rather than a violation of the inequality.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The classical Gabor inequality is tight for an untruncated Gaussian
carrier in continuous time.  The digital atom \eqref{eq:atom} truncates
the Gaussian to a 40\,ms rectangular support.  Truncation adds power in
the tails of the frequency distribution, increasing $\sigma_f$ beyond the
ideal $1/(4\pi\sigma_t)$.  The measured overshoot of $\approx\GaborOvershoot$
is consistent with this effect at $\sigma = 8$\,ms within a 40\,ms window
(approximately $2.5\sigma$ half-width, retaining $\approx 98.8\%$ of
Gaussian energy).  The inequality $\sigma_t \cdot \sigma_f \ge 1/(4\pi)$
holds by the Cauchy-Schwarz argument on $L^2(\mathbb{R})$ regardless of
truncation; the question is only how close to tight the digital realisation
falls.
\end{proof}

\section{Corollary: Tonotopic Equivalence}
\label{sec:corollary}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\begin{corollary}[Tonotopic equivalence]\label{cor:tonotopic}
Let $\delta_{\mathrm{FFT}}(a)$ and $\delta_{\mathrm{coch}}(a)$ denote the
trit identity returned by, respectively, a discrete Fourier magnitude
decoder and a gammatone cochlear filterbank applied to atom $a$.  For all
atoms produced by the MCORE-1 synthesis chain,
$\delta_{\mathrm{FFT}}(a) = \delta_{\mathrm{coch}}(a)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The three carriers occupy $f_0=800$\,Hz, $f_1=1600$\,Hz, $f_2=3200$\,Hz.
The effective $-3$\,dB bandwidth of a Gabor atom with $\sigma=8$\,ms is
$B \approx 1/(2\pi\sigma) \approx 20$\,Hz.  The minimum frequency
separation is $f_1 - f_0 = 800$\,Hz, giving a guard ratio of
$800 / 20 = 40$.  Under any filterbank whose adjacent channel crossover
is below $-30$\,dB at a separation of 800\,Hz---a condition satisfied by
standard gammatone filterbanks \cite{patterson1992auditory} and by the
FFT magnitude decoder---the peak-energy channel is the same regardless of
the decoder.  Hence both decoders assign the same trit label.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Biological significance]
Cochlear place coding is the primary frequency-discrimination mechanism
of the auditory periphery \cite{vonbekesy1960experiments}.  Corollary~\ref{cor:tonotopic}
states that a cochlear implant or BCI delivering trit-encoded genomic
information via tonotopic frequency channels would decode correctly with
zero algorithmic overhead: the cochlea's place map is an equivalent
decoder for this atom set.  This is not a psychoacoustic claim about
perceptual discrimination thresholds; it is a statement about the
separation geometry of the synthesis basis.
\end{remark}

Empirically, \TonotopicAgreement\% agreement was observed across all
2041 synthesis atoms ($681+680+680$ trits from wildtype, c.35delG, and
c.235delC).

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Discussion}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{Why p $\approx$ 1 is the correct baseline}

The global chi-square non-significance (Section~\ref{sec:gjb2}) is not a
null result.  A marginal histogram integrates over all 681 positions:
removing one base perturbs the count of one symbol and compresses the
total length by one.  The resulting frequency shift is $O(1/n)$, invisible
under any reasonable significance threshold.  Reporting this as the
primary finding would be misleading; reporting it as an expected
calibration point---and then showing the sequential signal---is the
correct framing.

\subsection{Carry as hidden state}

The $\approx$\DensAfterCThirtyFive{} downstream mismatch rate is not a
peculiarity of GJB2.  It is a direct consequence of Theorem~\ref{thm:cascade}:
the carry register is a form of sequential memory, and any perturbation
at position $k$ propagates that memory forward through the rest of the
sequence.  Binary encoders without an explicit carry register cannot
reproduce this behaviour without explicitly tracking an equivalent carry
register; the divergence is less compactly observable in a binary
readout.

\subsection{Prior work in genomic sonification}

Previous genomic sonification work has mapped bases to musical pitches
\cite{temple2017dna} or used pitch detuning to indicate genetic load
\cite{martin2024genomic}.  Neither approach encodes the sequential
carry structure exploited here, nor do they produce signals diagnostic of
frameshift position from downstream trit density alone.

\subsection{Limitations}

Prefix alignment with independent re-encoding is a reproducible convention,
not a claim about in-vivo reading-frame repair.  The Corollary holds for
the specific carrier triplet 800/1600/3200\,Hz; a different choice could
reduce guard bands and break the equivalence.  The checker implementation
in \texttt{mcore-1} covers tree depths up to 5; behaviour at larger depths
is an open question (Section~\ref{sec:open}).

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Open Questions}
\label{sec:open}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Detection depth $d^*$.}  Theorem~\ref{thm:cascade}(iii)
    guarantees the shallowest error at depth 1.  What is the distribution
    of the deepest error as a function of sequence statistics and tree arity?
  \item \textbf{Converse.}  Does every CONSERVATION error under post-order
    checking imply a deletion strictly inside the leaf span?  A formal
    converse would characterise the checker's false-positive rate under
    insertion and substitution perturbations.
  \item \textbf{Overshoot characterisation.}  The observed $\GaborOvershoot$-fold
    overshoot in $\sigma_t \cdot \sigma_f$ arises from finite-window
    effects.  A closed-form correction as a function of $\sigma / T_w$
    (Gaussian width relative to window length) would make the bound
    quantitatively tight.
  \item \textbf{$T$-bias necessity.}  Characterise $\epsilon_T$ as the
    minimiser of a stated conservation or decoding-error functional, or
    document empirical inferiority of symmetric breaks at $A$, $C$, and
    $G$ under the GJB2 controls.
  \item \textbf{Carry-sensitive observables.}  Identify statistics beyond
    independent-prefix $\rho$ for which carry-on and carry-off trajectories
    diverge (e.g.\ compositional encoding, re-start carry mid-sequence).
  \item \textbf{Tonotopic bandwidth robustness.}  At what minimum carrier
    separation does Corollary~\ref{cor:tonotopic} fail?  An ERB sweep from
    20 to 200\,Hz would characterise the margin.
  \item \textbf{Repository integration.}  The \texttt{mcore-1} tree checker
    and the \texttt{gjb2-mcore-sonification} encoder are currently separate
    codebases.  A unified pipeline would allow the checker to operate
    directly on GJB2 trit trees and produce Figure~\ref{fig:carry}
    via Theorem~\ref{thm:cascade} rather than post-hoc delta analysis.
\end{enumerate}

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Conclusion}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

MCORE-1 carries sequential variant information in a channel that binary
nucleotide encoders do not natively expose: reproducing the same
downstream sensitivity requires explicit auxiliary state and a chosen
lift from $\{0,1\}$ to trit-plus-carry dynamics.  For the two classical GJB2 deletions, global trit
histograms are indistinguishable from wildtype---as they must be---while
carry-mediated trit trajectories diverge across the majority of downstream
positions.  The synthesis atoms satisfy the Gabor uncertainty bound, and
cochlear place coding is confirmed as an equivalent decoder for this
carrier geometry, adding biological grounding to the acoustic
representation.  Together, these three results describe a
self-consistent framework: algebraic carry (Theorem~\ref{thm:cascade}),
physical basis (Theorem~\ref{thm:gabor}), and perceptual equivalence
(Corollary~\ref{cor:tonotopic}).
