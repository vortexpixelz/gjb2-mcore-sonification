\title{Ternary Encoding of Genomic Variants as Audible Gabor Atoms: A MCORE-1 Framework Applied to GJB2 Hearing Loss Mutations}

\author{Jacob Walker}
\address{Symonic LLC, Simpsonville, SC, United States}

\begin{abstract}
Binary clinical genomics compresses molecular evidence into discrete calls---wildtype versus pathogenic, present versus absent---and in doing so discards conjugate structure that is not representable in a two-state measurement basis. This work treats DNA as a discrete signal, encodes it in the MCORE-1 ternary algebra with carry-augmented dynamics, and maps each emitted trit to a minimum-uncertainty Gabor atom at 48\,kHz. For NM\_004004.6 \textit{GJB2} and two recurrent one-base deletions (c.35delG and c.235delC), global trit histograms are statistically indistinguishable from wildtype ($\chi^2=\ChiSqCThirtyFive$, $p=\ensuremath{\PCThirtyFive}$; $\chi^2=\ChiSqCTwoThirtyFive$, $p=\ensuremath{\PCTwoThirtyFive}$; df$=2$), which is expected when a single base is removed from a 681-bp coding sequence: the marginal $\{0,1,2\}$ frequencies barely move. The scientifically informative signal is positional: prefix-aligned wildtype versus mutant trit streams first diverge at the lesion (c.35delG: position \FirstDiffCThirtyFive; c.235delC: position \FirstDiffCTwoThirtyFive) and then disagree across most downstream sites (after the deletion coordinate, $\DiffAfterCThirtyFive/\TailAfterCThirtyFive$ and $\DiffAfterCTwoThirtyFive/\TailAfterCTwoThirtyFive$ mismatching positions; mean mismatch densities $\DensAfterCThirtyFive$ and $\DensAfterCTwoThirtyFive$). A localized 10-trit window centered on each deletion yields Pearson homogeneity statistics $\chi^2=\WinChiCThirtyFive$ ($p=\ensuremath{\WinPCThirtyFive}$) and $\chi^2=\WinChiCTwoThirtyFive$ ($p=\ensuremath{\WinPCTwoThirtyFive}$), i.e., a small-sample frequency contrast that need not capture the full trajectory divergence. Thus MCORE-1 encodes variant impact primarily through carry-mediated trajectory divergence---a sequential object that binary nucleotide parity discards unless an explicit carry track is adjoined. Gabor atoms used for synthesis satisfy the stated time-frequency uncertainty bound. Together, these results reframe ``$p\approx 1$'' at the histogram level as an anticipated baseline, while highlighting cascade-rich ternary dynamics as the locus of variant-specific structure.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Clinical and screening genomics increasingly relies on categorical variant interpretation: a site is called pathogenic or benign, a carrier status is present or absent, an allele is detected or not. This binarization is operationally necessary, but it is not information-neutral. Many biochemical degrees of freedom---rates, occupancies, cooperative transitions---are projected away when the final readout is forced into two labels. The MCORE-1 construction used here is motivated by a narrower and more formal claim: a three-valued algebraic state can represent carry-augmented structure in a DNA scan that a bit-wise alphabet cannot preserve without auxiliary bookkeeping.

GJB2 (Connexin 26) is an appropriate stress test for any ``extra state'' hypothesis because it is both medically central and densely characterized: common recessive alleles recur at high frequency in defined populations, and the reference coding sequence is available from public databases \citep{lee2009connexin26,mese2007gap,ncbi_nm004004}. The present manuscript does not assert clinical utility; it asks a preliminary signal-processing question: if DNA is encoded into a ternary stream and mapped into Gabor atoms, what statistical signatures appear at the level of (i) global trit histograms, (ii) localized windows around pathogenic breakpoints, (iii) carry-mediated downstream divergence between aligned trit trajectories, and (iv) time-frequency localization of the synthesis primitives?

Time-frequency analysis is not imported for metaphor. Gabor's theory of communication establishes Gaussian-modulated complex exponentials as the unique family that saturates the classical bandwidth-duration product \citep{gabor1946theory}. The same inequality class appears in quantum measurement, where non-commuting observables impose joint-resolution limits \citep{heisenberg1927anschaulichen}. Human listeners can, under some paradigms, outperform naive Fourier-style bounds \citep{oppenheim2013human}; the point for this work is more conservative: the sonification basis is chosen so that each trit label is realized as a band-limited transient whose second moments are internally consistent with a stated uncertainty bound.

\section{Theoretical framework}

\subsection{MCORE-1 trit algebra}

Let the DNA alphabet be scanned 5$'$ to 3$'$. MCORE-1 assigns base values in $\mathbb{Z}$ and propagates a carry. With base values $A\!\rightarrow\!0$, $C\!\rightarrow\!1$, $G\!\rightarrow\!2$, and $T\!\rightarrow\!0$ plus an additional increment that implements a $T$-biased carry, each base contributes a provisional sum that is reduced modulo 3 to emit a trit in $\{0,1,2\}$ while updating the carry for the next position. Concretely, if $v(b)$ is the integer base value, $c_i$ is the carry entering position $i$, and $\epsilon_T(b)$ is 1 if $b=T$ and 0 otherwise, then
\begin{equation}
  u_i = v(b_i) + c_i + \epsilon_T(b_i), \qquad t_i = u_i \bmod 3, \qquad c_{i+1} = \left\lfloor u_i/3\right\rfloor .
\end{equation}
The emitted sequence $(t_i)$ is the object mapped to audio. Algebraicly, the scan is a joint dynamics between a visible trit and a hidden carry; pathogenic edits that preserve base composition can still perturb the carry trajectory and therefore the emitted trits.

\subsection{Gabor limit in acoustic space}

For a square-integrable pulse $g(t)$, define time and frequency spreads via second moments of energy. With appropriate normalization, the classical inequality can be written as
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_t \,\sigma_f \;\ge\; \frac{1}{4\pi},
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_t$ and $\sigma_f$ are root-mean-squared durations in seconds and Hertz. Gaussian envelopes modulated by a carrier achieve equality in the continuum idealization. Digital synthesis at finite sample rate introduces discretization; the verification reported below therefore uses zero-padding to approximate continuum bandwidth while keeping the underlying waveform identical.

\subsection{Why Gabor atoms}

Gabor atoms are mathematically distinguished as minimizers (or near-minimizers under digital constraints) of joint time-frequency uncertainty for a fixed bandwidth budget. If a genomic alphabet is to be rendered as sound without arbitrary choice of window and carrier, Gaussians provide a standard, physically interpretable bridge between discrete sequence data and continuous waveforms.

\subsection{Delta representation}

Given wildtype and mutant DNA strings, this study compares MCORE-1 trits after independent re-encoding of an aligned prefix of length $n=\min(L_{\mathrm{wt}},L_{\mathrm{mut}})$. Position $i\le n$ uses the wildtype base $b^{\mathrm{wt}}_i$ and mutant base $b^{\mathrm{mut}}_i$ with carries restarted at zero for each sequence, yielding trits $t^{\mathrm{wt}}_i$ and $t^{\mathrm{mut}}_i$. The modular delta is
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_i = \bigl(t^{\mathrm{mut}}_i - t^{\mathrm{wt}}_i\bigr) \bmod 3 .
\end{equation}
This definition is honest about deletion: after a one-base deletion, there is no canonical base-to-base alignment across the full CDS; the prefix convention fixes a definite, reproducible comparison window.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Reference sequence}

NM\_004004.6 was retrieved from NCBI using E-utilities \citep{ncbi_nm004004}. The coding sequence was extracted from the GenBank CDS annotation (join 179..859 on the RefSeq record as retrieved in 2026), yielding 681 coding bases beginning with ATG.

\subsection{Variants}

We modeled c.35delG (rs80338943) and c.235delC (rs80338939) as single-nucleotide deletions at the corresponding 1-indexed CDS coordinates \citep{clinvar_rs80338943,clinvar_rs80338939}. These variants are recurrent in population genetics studies of hearing loss; clinical interpretation is not revisited here.

\subsection{Trit encoding and audio synthesis}

Encoding, deletion application, and waveform rendering follow the reference implementation in \texttt{code/gjb2\_sonification.py}. Audio is generated at 48\,kHz with 40\,ms per trit and an 8\,ms Gaussian $\sigma$, mapping trits $\{0,1,2\}$ to carrier frequencies $\{800,1600,3200\}$\,Hz.

\subsection{Statistical analysis}

For each sequence, MCORE-1 trits were counted in $\{0,1,2\}$. For each variant, Pearson's chi-square goodness-of-fit test compared observed trit counts to expected counts proportional to the wildtype distribution, scaled to the mutant length (680 trits after a one-base deletion). Degrees of freedom are 2; significance is reported at $\alpha=0.05$ \citep{agresti2013categorical}.

\subsection{Positional window contrast}

For each variant, a length-10 window of trits was taken along the prefix-aligned wildtype and mutant streams, centered on the clinical deletion coordinate in wildtype numbering (clamped so the window lies entirely within the 680 overlapping positions). Pearson's chi-square test of homogeneity was applied to the resulting $2\times 3$ contingency table (wildtype window versus mutant window), df$=2$.

\subsection{Carry propagation metrics}

Still under prefix alignment with independent re-encoding from the sequence start, we compared wildtype and mutant trit strings $t^{\mathrm{wt}}_i$ and $t^{\mathrm{mut}}_i$ and recorded (a) the first index $i$ with $t^{\mathrm{wt}}_i\neq t^{\mathrm{mut}}_i$, (b) the total mismatch count, (c) the mismatch count strictly downstream of the 1-indexed deletion coordinate, and (d) that downstream count divided by the number of compared positions after the deletion site (tail length $681-k$ for deletion at $k$). A rolling mean (width 10) of the downstream mismatch indicator was plotted to summarize spatial density.

\subsection{Delta density}

Prefix-aligned deltas $\Delta_i$ were converted to a binary indicator $I_i=\mathbb{1}[\Delta_i\neq 0]$ and smoothed with a length-10 moving average to visualize local density of mutation-affected trits along the CDS index $i$.

\subsection{Gabor uncertainty check}

For each trit label, the discrete \texttt{gabor\_click} waveform was energy-normalized, zero-padded to a 2\,s window at 48\,kHz, and second moments $\sigma_t$ and $\sigma_f$ were computed in time and frequency from the padded energy distribution. Products $\sigma_t\sigma_f$ were compared to $1/(4\pi)$.

\subsection{Reproducibility}

Code, figures, and audio are available at \url{https://github.com/vortexpixelz/gjb2-mcore-sonification}. Running \texttt{python code/analysis.py} regenerates the figure files and the LaTeX fragments under \texttt{paper/figures/}.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Trit distributions}

Figure~\ref{fig:trit} summarizes trit frequencies for wildtype and both deletions. The global chi-square tests do not reject the null of identical multinomial proportions for either variant at $\alpha=0.05$ (c.35delG: $\chi^2=\ChiSqCThirtyFive$, $p=\ensuremath{\PCThirtyFive}$; c.235delC: $\chi^2=\ChiSqCTwoThirtyFive$, $p=\ensuremath{\PCTwoThirtyFive}$). This near-invariance is expected: removing one base from 681 should not materially change aggregate trit frequencies. Table~\ref{tab:summary} lists the underlying counts.

We next examined a 10-trit window centered on each deletion site (wildtype indices \WinLoCThirtyFive--\WinHiCThirtyFive{} and \WinLoCTwoThirtyFive--\WinHiCTwoThirtyFive). Pearson homogeneity tests on the $2\times 3$ window tables yield $\chi^2=\WinChiCThirtyFive$ ($p=\ensuremath{\WinPCThirtyFive}$) and $\chi^2=\WinChiCTwoThirtyFive$ ($p=\ensuremath{\WinPCTwoThirtyFive}$). With only ten emitted trits per channel, this local frequency contrast has limited power; the more striking effect is sequential.

\subsection{Carry cascade in aligned trit streams}

Under prefix alignment, the first trit mismatch occurs at position \FirstDiffCThirtyFive{} for c.35delG and \FirstDiffCTwoThirtyFive{} for c.235delC. Total mismatches are \TotalDiffCThirtyFive{} and \TotalDiffCTwoThirtyFive{} out of 680 compared positions. Downstream of the deletion coordinate, mismatches persist at high density: \DiffAfterCThirtyFive/\TailAfterCThirtyFive{} ($\DensAfterCThirtyFive$) and \DiffAfterCTwoThirtyFive/\TailAfterCTwoThirtyFive{} ($\DensAfterCTwoThirtyFive$). \section{Discussion}

The headline ``$p\approx 1$'' result is therefore not a failed experiment; it is the correct answer to an intentionally coarse question. Global trit histograms integrate over hundreds of positions: a single-nucleotide deletion perturbs only one input symbol, so marginal $\{0,1,2\}$ frequencies need not move detectably under multinomial sampling. Interpreting that outcome as ``no signal'' would confuse a sufficient statistic with the underlying dynamical object.

The signal is local and sequential. MCORE-1 is not memoryless: the emitted trit at site $i$ is a function of the entire upstream DNA through the carry register. A deletion shifts every downstream base index; even when the deleted column sometimes yields the same emitted trit by coincidence, the carry trajectory can desynchronize, producing long runs of downstream disagreement. In the present data, roughly three-fifths of positions after the deletion coordinate exhibit mismatched trits between aligned streams---far beyond the $\mathcal{O}(1)$ footprint of the DNA edit itself. That cascade is the sense in which a third algebraic state (trit plus carry dynamics) encodes variant position information that a naive binary nucleotide parity bit does not carry unless one explicitly models the same hidden carry.

The 10-trit window chi-square is a deliberately local frequency probe around each breakpoint. It can remain non-significant even when trajectory-level divergence is large, because ten symbols provide limited evidence about a three-category histogram and because the breakpoint need not coincide with the largest local count skew. Interpreting results requires both panels: histogram invariance plus downstream mismatch density (Figures~\ref{fig:trit}, \ref{fig:carry}, and \ref{fig:delta}).

The acoustic mapping is best understood as a measurement model: each emitted trit selects a band-limited atom in a separated frequency triplet (800/1600/3200\,Hz). The uncertainty verification ties that choice to standard time-frequency physics rather than ad hoc sound design.

Limitations are explicit. Prefix alignment with independent re-encoding is a reproducible convention, not a claim about reading-frame repair in vivo. MCORE-1 carry is sensitive to boundary conditions. This is a single-gene demonstration. Finally, human auditory performance can violate naive Fourier uncertainty benchmarks in psychophysical tasks \citep{oppenheim2013human}; the present construction is about synthesis basis properties, not perceptual discrimination thresholds.

The carry cascade observed here is not a bug of the encoding---it is the feature. A single-nucleotide deletion propagates through $\sim$61\% of downstream trit positions because carry state is a form of sequential memory. Binary encodings of the same deletion carry no such downstream signal without explicit auxiliary state. This is precisely the $S_3$ term: information present in the ternary representation that binary measurement architecturally excludes.

\section{Conclusion}

MCORE-1 trit encoding combined with Gabor-atom synthesis provides a reproducible, physically grounded route from DNA edits to audio. For the two classic GJB2 deletions studied here, aggregate trit frequencies stay near wildtype while carry-mediated trajectory divergence produces extensive downstream disagreement in aligned trit streams. That separation between marginal histogram invariance and sequential sensitivity is the main empirical point: ternary measurement can represent variant impact in a dynamical channel that binary summaries do not natively expose.
